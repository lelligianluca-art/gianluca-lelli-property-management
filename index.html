Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from foreign investors considering short-term rental apartments in Italy.

Can foreign investors buy apartments in Italy for short-term rentals?

Yes. Non-residents can purchase residential property in Italy. After purchase you must comply with local short-term rental rules, including CIN/CIR registration, tourist tax and guest reporting. A local partner makes the process significantly easier.

What returns can I expect from a short-term rental apartment in Rome?

Returns depend on location, size, condition and management quality. Well-located apartments in central or well-connected areas of Rome can generate attractive occupancy and nightly rates compared with long-term leases. A realistic, data-based evaluation is essential before buying.

Do I need to live in Italy to run a short-term rental?

No. Many foreign owners operate successfully from abroad with a trusted local manager who handles guest communication, cleaning, check-in, maintenance and legal compliance.

What is CIN and CIR registration?

CIN (Codice Identificativo Nazionale) and CIR (Codice Identificativo Regionale) are mandatory identification codes for short-term rental properties in Italy. Proper registration is required to list legally on platforms such as Airbnb and Booking.com.
